Lagrangian Mechanics Primer (20 min)

Lagrange Multipliers: A Primer

Problem from Lecture 3:

  • Dynamics: \(\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = -G(\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\theta}\)
  • Assumes marginal entropy conservation “just works”
  • Need general framework for explicit constraints

Solution: Lagrange multipliers

Review: Constrained Optimisation

Standard Lagrange Multipliers: \[ \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x}, \lambda) = f(\mathbf{x}) + \lambda(g(\mathbf{x}) - c) \]

Optimality conditions:

  • \(\nabla f + \lambda \nabla g = 0\) (gradient perpendicular to constraint)
  • \(g(\mathbf{x}) = c\) (constraint satisfied)

Geometric Interpretation

Geometric Picture:

Gradient \(\nabla f\) perpendicular to constraint surface

  • If not perpendicular → could improve \(f\) while staying on constraint
  • Multiplier \(\lambda\) measures “strength” of constraint

From Optimisation to Dynamics

Constraint Maintenance: \[ \sum h_i(\boldsymbol{\theta}(t)) = C \quad \forall t \]

Implicit form: \[ a(\boldsymbol{\theta})^\top \dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = 0 \]

where \(a(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \sum_i \nabla h_i\)

Stay on constraint surface by moving tangent to it

Tangent Space Projection

Tangent Space Projector: \[ \Pi_\parallel = I - \frac{aa^\top}{a^\top a} \]

Properties:

  • \(\Pi_\parallel a = 0\) (kills normal component)
  • \(\Pi_\parallel^2 = \Pi_\parallel\) (idempotent)
  • If \(a^\top v = 0\) then \(\Pi_\parallel v = v\)

Projects any vector onto tangent space

Lagrangian for Dynamics

Information Lagrangian: \[ \mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \nu) = -H(\boldsymbol{\theta}) + \nu\left(\sum h_i - C\right) \]

  • \(-H\): Minimize negative entropy = maximize entropy
  • \(\nu\): Lagrange multiplier (time-dependent!)
  • Constraint: \(\sum h_i = C\)

\(\nu(t)\) adjusts to maintain constraint during evolution

Constrained Gradient Flow

Constrained Dynamics: \[ \dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = -G(\boldsymbol{\theta})\boldsymbol{\theta} - \nu(t) a(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \]

  • First term: MEP (entropy increase)
  • Second term: Constraint force (maintains \(\sum h_i = C\))
  • \(\nu(t)\): Adjusts to enforce constraint

Two competing forces: increase entropy vs. stay on manifold

Why Constraint Maintenance Works

Constraint Maintenance:

Require: \(a^\top \dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = 0\)

\[ \nu(t) = -\frac{a^\top G\boldsymbol{\theta}}{a^\top a} \]

Result: \[ \dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = -\Pi_\parallel G\boldsymbol{\theta} \]

Projected entropy gradient stays on manifold

Summary: Lagrangian Formulation

Key Results:

  • Constrained dynamics: \(\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = -G\boldsymbol{\theta} - \nu a\)
  • Multiplier: \(\nu = -\frac{a^\top G\boldsymbol{\theta}}{a^\top a}\) (automatic!)
  • Geometric: \(\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = -\Pi_\parallel G\boldsymbol{\theta}\) (projected)

Computing \(\nu(t)\) (15 min)

Dynamics of the Lagrange Multiplier \(\nu(t)\)

Lagrange Multiplier: \[ \nu(t) = -\frac{a^\top G\boldsymbol{\theta}}{a^\top a} \]

Questions:

  • What does \(\nu\) represent?
  • How does it evolve?
  • What controls its magnitude?

Physical Interpretation of \(\nu\)

Three Interpretations:

  1. Constraint force: Magnitude of \(-\nu a\) term
  2. Shadow price: Cost of enforcing constraint
  3. Tension: How hard constraint “pulls”

All equivalent—different physical pictures

Evolution of \(\nu(t)\)

Evolution: \[ \dot{\nu} = \frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\left(-\frac{a^\top G\boldsymbol{\theta}}{a^\top a}\right) \]

  • \(|\nu|\) large: Constraint strongly active
  • \(|\nu|\) small: Nearly compatible flow
  • \(\nu = 0\): Stationary point (equilibrium)

Geometric Picture: Angle Between Gradients

Geometric Formula: \[ \nu \propto -\frac{\cos\theta}{\|a\|} \] where \(\theta\) = angle between \(-G\boldsymbol{\theta}\) and \(a\)

  • Small angle: Entropy pulls off surface \(\rightarrow\) large \(|\nu|\)
  • Near 90°: Entropy tangent to surface \(\rightarrow\) small \(|\nu|\)

Computing \(\nu(t)\) in Practice: A Major Computational Challenge

Computing \(\nu\): Exponentially Hard!

Need \(a = \sum_i \nabla h_i\) where each \(h_i\) requires marginalizing \(N-1\) variables

  • \(N\) binary variables → \(2^{N-1}\) operations per marginal!
  • Classical mechanics: \(\nabla E\) is trivial (linear/quadratic)
  • Information: \(\nabla(\sum h_i)\) is exponentially expensive

Only tractable in: * Gaussian regime (closed form) * Strong independence structure

Nonlinear constraints are much harder than linear ones

Example: Simple Two-Variable System

Two-Variable Example:

  • Parameters: \((\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_{12})\)
  • Constraint: \(h_1 + h_2 = C\)
  • Gradient: \(a \approx (\partial h_1/\partial\theta_1, \partial h_2/\partial\theta_2, 0)\)

Interaction \(\theta_{12}\) is “unconstrained” direction

\(\nu\) large when marginals changing fast

Sign of \(\nu\) and Direction of Constraint Force

Sign of \(\nu\):

  • \(\nu > 0\): Force opposes increasing \(\sum h_i\)
  • \(\nu < 0\): Force opposes decreasing \(\sum h_i\)
  • \(\nu = 0\): Stationary point (no force needed)

Constraint maintains \(\sum h_i = C\) from both sides

Connection to Hamiltonian Structure

Preview: Hamiltonian Connection

  • Constraint defines submanifold
  • \(\nu\) related to conjugate momentum
  • Poisson bracket structure (Lecture 5)
  • GENERIC framework (Lecture 8)

Lagrange multipliers bridge optimization and Hamiltonian mechanics

Summary: Understanding \(\nu(t)\)

Key Points:

  • \(\nu\) auto-determined by constraint
  • Magnitude: strength of enforcement
  • Sign: direction of force
  • \(\nu = 0\): equilibrium

Bridge between gradient flow and constrained geometry

Stationary Points and Equilibria (15 min)

Stationary Points and Equilibria

Question: When does \(\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = 0\)?

Answer: At equilibria—stationary points of constrained dynamics

Condition for Stationary Points

Stationary Condition: \[ G\boldsymbol{\theta} + \nu a = 0 \]

Two cases:

  1. \(\nu = 0\): Maximum entropy (\(\boldsymbol{\theta} = 0\))
  2. \(\nu \neq 0\): Constrained equilibrium

Constraint balances entropy gradient

Maximum Entropy Equilibrium

Maximum Entropy Equilibrium:

  • \(\boldsymbol{\theta} = 0\) (reference measure, no bias)
  • \(I = 0\) (independent variables)
  • \(h_i = C/N\) (equal marginals)
  • Maximum \(H\), minimum \(I\)

Thermal equilibrium of information

Constrained Equilibria

Constrained Equilibria:

At equilibrium: \(\boldsymbol{\theta} = -\nu G^{-1} a\)

Plus constraint: \(\sum h_i(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = C\)

Two equations, two unknowns (\(\boldsymbol{\theta}\), \(\nu\))

Generally requires numerical solution

Local vs. Global Equilibria

Types of Equilibria:

  • Global max: \(\boldsymbol{\theta} = 0\) (\(H\) strictly concave)
  • Constrained: \(\nu \neq 0\) (all should be stable!)
  • Critical slowing: Small eigenvalues \(\rightarrow\) slow modes

No true saddles if \(H\) strictly concave and \(G\) positive definite

Slow modes from small eigenvalues, not instability

Stability Analysis

Stability:

Linearised: \(\delta\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \approx -\Pi_\parallel G \delta\boldsymbol{\theta}\)

Since \(G\) positive definite \(\rightarrow\) all eigenvalues negative

  • Large \(|\lambda|\): Fast convergence
  • Small \(|\lambda|\): Critical slowing
  • Zero: Constraint direction

All equilibria stable; speed varies by mode

Example: Two-Variable System

Two-Variable Example:

Max entropy (\(C = 2\log 2\)): * \(h_1 = h_2 = \log 2\) (uniform) * \(I = 0\) (independent) * \(\nu = 0\)

Constrained (\(C < 2\log 2\)): * \(h_1, h_2 < \log 2\) (biased) * \(I > 0\) (correlated) * \(\nu \neq 0\)

Critical Slowing Near Equilibria

Critical Slowing:

Near equilibrium: \(\|\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\| \to 0\)

Consequences:

  • Long relaxation times
  • Slow modes with small eigenvalues
  • Emergent timescale hierarchy

Different modes relax at different rates

Equilibria and Regime Structure

Regime Structure:

  • Basins of attraction \(\rightarrow\) regimes
  • Timescale hierarchy \(\rightarrow\) emergence
  • Slow modes \(\rightarrow\) long-lived structures

Equilibria organize the whole dynamical landscape

  • Maximum entropy: \(\boldsymbol{\theta} = 0\) (global)
